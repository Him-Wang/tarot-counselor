# Card Relationships

How cards speak to each other: elements, numbers, major/minor correspondence, and how to weave these into a reading.

## 1. The four elements (wands / cups / swords / pentacles)
- Fire (wands): desire, action, creativity — "what do I want and what will I do?"
- Water (cups): feeling, relationships, intuition — "what do I feel, and what do I need?"
- Air (swords): thought, words, conflict — "what is true, and how is it being said?"
- Earth (pentacles): body, work, money — "what is real and sustainable?"

Interaction of elements:
- Fire + Water: inspiration meets feeling (creative warmth, or steam and conflict).
- Fire + Air: idea becomes drive (bold plans, or rapid words).
- Water + Earth: feeling made practical (care in action, or emotional stagnation).
- Air + Earth: thought applied to matter (planning, or over-analysis).
- Same element repeated: the theme is amplified; check whether the reading is one-dimensional.
- All four present: the situation engages every layer of life.

## 2. Numbers 1–10: stages of a cycle
- Ace: origin, seed, possible beginning.
- Two: polarity and choice; the first relationship.
- Three: expression, expansion, first result.
- Four: structure, stability, a resting point.
- Five: conflict and testing of the structure.
- Six: adjustment, movement toward balance.
- Seven: challenge, defence, re-evaluation.
- Eight: momentum and swift change.
- Nine: culmination, near-completion, strain before the end.
- Ten: completion and what comes after (often a return to a new Ace).

## 3. Major arcana and numbered minors: the shared numbers
The numbered minors echo the major arcana at the same number (mod 22 when useful):
- 1 Magician ↔ Aces (raw potential given form)
- 2 High Priestess ↔ Twos (polarity, hidden knowledge)
- 3 Empress ↔ Threes (growth, creativity)
- 4 Emperor ↔ Fours (structure and order)
- 5 Hierophant ↔ Fives (testing, tradition, conflict)
- 6 Lovers ↔ Sixes (harmony and choice)
- 7 Chariot ↔ Sevens (will and challenge)
- 8 Strength ↔ Eights (power in motion)
- 9 Hermit ↔ Nines (solitude, fruition, discernment)
- 10 Wheel of Fortune ↔ Tens (cycle complete, change returns)
Use this as a resonance, not a rigid rule: "The Six of Cups and the Lovers both speak of a heart-level choice."

## 4. Court cards versus numbered cards
- Numbered cards describe events, phases, and situations.
- Court cards describe people, attitudes, or a mode of relating (Page/Princess, Knight, Queen, King).
- In "advice" positions, a court card can name a quality to embody: the Queen of Swords = clarity and honesty; the Knight of Pentacles = steady diligence.

## 5. Narrative reading: linking cards into a story
Read adjacency as a sentence: subject → verb → object. Example: "The Fool (new beginning) + Three of Wands (expansion) + Six of Pentacles (exchange)" becomes "a fresh start grows into wider plans, and the resources will need to be shared."
Check for:
- Repeating suits (dominant life area), repeating numbers (stage), repeating majors (archetypal theme).
- Contrast (fire/water, major/minor) — where is the tension, where is the support?
- A single card that "doesn't fit" often names the real obstacle.

## 6. Suit progression as a journey
Think of each suit as climbing a mountain:
1–3: leaving base camp toward a goal, discovering the landscape.
4–6: building a camp, meeting others, adjusting to the terrain.
7–9: the steep climb — challenge, momentum, near the summit.
10: the summit and the view; then a new mountain calls (Ace).

## 7. Boundaries
- These correspondences are aids to storytelling, not fixed laws.
- Never override the querent's own account with a symbolic correspondence.
- Say "this pattern may suggest…" rather than "this means…".
