# Major Arcana — RWS Meanings

Each entry lists card number, keywords, core theme, upright and reversed meanings, and card symbolism. Standard Rider–Waite–Smith English names are used.

## The Fool
- Keywords: new beginning, spontaneity, trust, adventure
- Number: 0 — the leap before the journey
- Core: The Fool steps into the unknown with open hands. A call to begin with trust rather than certainty.
- Symbolism: a young traveller at a cliff edge, a small dog, a white rose, a bindle, a yellow sky; the cliff suggests risk, the dog loyalty, the rose purity of intention.
- Upright: A fresh start, a leap of faith, curiosity and freedom. You may be entering new territory without a map; stay open and learn as you go.
- Reversed: Recklessness or fear of the unknown. Either you rush in without a plan, or you hold back from a needed beginning. Discernment before the leap.

## The Magician
- Keywords: will, action, skill, manifestation, resources
- Number: 1 — conscious creation
- Core: The Magician channels intention into action. The tools are already on the table; what is missing is the decision to use them.
- Symbolism: the lemniscate above the head, one hand raised to sky and one to earth, the four suits on the table, a serpent belt; the gesture says "as above, so below."
- Upright: You have the skills and resources to make something happen. Focus your will, communicate clearly, and take the first concrete step.
- Reversed: Manipulation, scattered energy, or blocked power. Talent used without integrity, or a sense of powerlessness. Recheck motives and use of influence.

## The High Priestess
- Keywords: intuition, mystery, inner knowing, the unconscious
- Number: 2 — receptive wisdom
- Core: The High Priestess guards the threshold between the seen and unseen. She asks for silence and listening rather than action.
- Symbolism: a veiled woman between two pillars (black and white), a crescent moon at her feet, a scroll, a pomegranate curtain; the veil hides what is not yet ready to be revealed.
- Upright: Trust intuition. The answer is within, though not yet fully spoken. Notice dreams, synchronicities, and quiet knowing; do not force disclosure.
- Reversed: Ignoring inner voice, hidden agendas, or secrets coming to light. Either you dismiss intuition or you are pulled into another's mystery. Return to stillness.

## The Empress
- Keywords: abundance, nurture, creativity, growth, the feminine
- Number: 3 — generative life
- Core: The Empress is the fertile ground where ideas and relationships grow. Care and attention bring abundance.
- Symbolism: a crown of twelve stars, a wheat field, a flowing robe, a shield with a Venus sign, a stream; all signs of life, comfort, and natural wealth.
- Upright: Nurture what matters. Creativity, pregnancy, prosperity, or a supportive environment. Good things grow with patience and care.
- Reversed: Neglect, creative block, over-giving, or dependence. The nurturing energy is blocked or one-sided. Restore balance between giving and receiving.

## The Emperor
- Keywords: structure, authority, discipline, protection, the masculine
- Number: 4 — form and order
- Core: The Emperor establishes boundaries and structure. Freedom needs a container to become real.
- Symbolism: a stone throne with ram heads, a red robe, an orb and sceptre, mountains in the background; stability, rulership, and measured power.
- Upright: Take charge. Clear rules, leadership, responsibility, and protection of what you value. Build something that lasts.
- Reversed: Rigidity, domination, or lack of control. Authority used harshly, or chaos where structure is needed. Soften without abandoning boundaries.

## The Hierophant
- Keywords: tradition, teaching, institutions, faith, guidance
- Number: 5 — inherited wisdom
- Core: The Hierophant is the bridge to tradition and established teaching. Learning from those who went before.
- Symbolism: a pope-like figure in red and blue robes, a triple crown, a crosier, two keys, two followers; religious or institutional authority and transmitted knowledge.
- Upright: Seek guidance from a teacher, a tradition, or a formal structure. Conformity has value here; ask what can be learned from the established path.
- Reversed: Questioning dogma, breaking with convention, or misleading authority. Old rules no longer fit. Find your own relationship to tradition.

## The Lovers
- Keywords: love, choice, union, values, alignment
- Number: 6 — the choice of the heart
- Core: The Lovers marks a meaningful choice and the union of two people or two parts of the self. Alignment with values is the real test.
- Symbolism: a man and woman before an angel, a fiery sun, a serpent in the tree, the other tree aflame; innocence, desire, and the choice between them.
- Upright: Love, harmony, and a decision made from the heart. Relationships deepen when both people act in line with their values.
- Reversed: Disharmony, conflicting values, indecision, or a choice avoided. A relationship or decision is out of alignment; name the true values at stake.

## The Chariot
- Keywords: willpower, direction, victory, self-control, movement
- Number: 7 — directed force
- Core: The Chariot is the will steering opposing forces toward a goal. Victory comes from self-mastery, not brute speed.
- Symbolism: a charioteer in a armoured car drawn by a black and a white sphinx, a starry canopy, a city behind; controlled movement and the harnessing of contradiction.
- Upright: Move forward with determination. You can win if you hold the reins. Set a direction and keep conflicting impulses under one purpose.
- Reversed: Loss of control, aimless drive, defeat, or internal conflict. Forces are pulling against each other. Regain the reins before pushing harder.

## Strength
- Keywords: courage, gentleness, patience, inner power, compassion
- Number: 8 — mastery through gentleness
- Core: Strength is not force but the quiet courage to meet the lion with an open hand. The soft approach is the strong one.
- Symbolism: a woman gently closing a lion's jaws, a lemniscate above her head, a flower garland, a white robe; calm dominion over instinct.
- Upright: Face the challenge with patience and compassion. You are stronger than you think; do not mistake gentleness for weakness.
- Reversed: Self-doubt, harshness, or force used where sensitivity is needed. Courage falters, or confidence turns to dominance. Return to quiet steadiness.

## The Hermit
- Keywords: solitude, introspection, guidance, patience, inner light
- Number: 9 — the light within
- Core: The Hermit withdraws to find an answer that cannot be heard in company. Solitude here is a search, not an escape.
- Symbolism: a cloaked figure on a mountain, a lantern holding a six-pointed star, a staff, a grey robe; withdrawal, discernment, and a lamp for others.
- Upright: Step back and look inward. Time alone, study, or the counsel of a wise guide. The lamp lights only the next step, not the whole road.
- Reversed: Isolation, secrecy, or refusing needed help. Solitude has become withdrawal from life. Reconnect without abandoning the insight gained.

## Wheel of Fortune
- Keywords: cycles, change, fate, turning point, timing
- Number: 10 — the turning wheel
- Core: The Wheel reminds us that life moves in cycles. What goes up will turn; the only stable thing is change itself.
- Symbolism: a great wheel with the four letters T-A-R-O and the words "ROTA TARO", a sphinx, a serpent, a rising and falling figure; the cycle of fortune and the fixed points within it.
- Upright: A turn of events, a timely change, or a cycle completing. Cooperate with the movement rather than resist it. Fortune favours the prepared.
- Reversed: Resistance to change, bad timing, or a cycle repeating. The wheel still turns; clinging makes the fall harder. Look for the lesson in the reversal.

## Justice
- Keywords: balance, truth, cause and effect, fairness, accountability
- Number: 11 — the scales
- Core: Justice weighs actions honestly. Choices have consequences, and truth must be faced without flinching.
- Symbolism: a seated figure holding upright sword and scales, a straight horizon, two pillars, a red robe; objective judgement and moral clarity.
- Upright: A fair outcome, an honest assessment, or a decision that must be made on principle. Give each side its due and accept the result.
- Reversed: Bias, avoidance, unfairness, or denial of responsibility. A situation is out of balance. Seek truth even when it is uncomfortable.

## The Hanged Man
- Keywords: pause, surrender, new perspective, suspension, sacrifice
- Number: 12 — the upside-down view
- Core: The Hanged Man gains wisdom by willingly stopping. What looks like delay is a change of perspective.
- Symbolism: a figure hung by one foot from a wooden cross, a halo, a calm face, legs crossed in a four; voluntary suspension and luminous insight.
- Upright: Stop and see things differently. A deliberate pause, a change of viewpoint, or letting go of control. The answer comes by reversing your assumptions.
- Reversed: Stalling, martyrdom, or resistance to a needed pause. You may be sacrificing without meaning, or refusing to look from another angle. Release the victim story.

## Death
- Keywords: endings, transformation, release, transition, renewal
- Number: 13 — necessary ending
- Core: Death is the end of a phase so that something new can begin. It nearly always means transformation, not literal death.
- Symbolism: a skeletal rider on a white horse with a black banner bearing a white rose, a rising sun between towers, a river; endings, purification, and the life that continues.
- Upright: An ending is due. Let go of what has finished, however uncomfortable. grief and transition are part of the passage to renewal.
- Reversed: Resistance to change, a delayed ending, or fear of loss. Clinging prolongs the dying phase. Accept what is already over.

## Temperance
- Keywords: balance, moderation, blending, patience, healing
- Number: 14 — the mixing of opposites
- Core: Temperance pours water between two cups, blending what seemed incompatible. Healing comes through measure and gradual integration.
- Symbolism: an angel with one foot in water and one on land, two gold cups, a path to distant mountains, a sun crown; harmony between elements and patient adjustment.
- Upright: Find the middle path. Blend opposing needs, go slowly, and keep adjusting. Good things take time to come together.
- Reversed: Excess, imbalance, impatience, or conflict that cannot be mixed. One side has taken over. Restore proportion rather than choosing a new extreme.

## The Devil
- Keywords: bondage, desire, materialism, shadow, compulsion
- Number: 15 — the chains we accept
- Core: The Devil shows the chains we mistake for safety. The bonds are loose; what keeps us is habit, fear, or desire.
- Symbolism: a horned figure on a pedestal, two naked captives with loose chains, an inverted pentagram, a torch; bondage that is chosen and therefore can be undone.
- Upright: Name the compulsion. Greed, addiction, unhealthy attachment, or a situation that feels binding. The first act of freedom is noticing the chains are loose.
- Reversed: Breaking free, confronting shadow, or loosening a habit. Release is underway, though it may be uncomfortable. Take back the power you gave away.

## The Tower
- Keywords: upheaval, revelation, sudden change, truth, clearing
- Number: 16 — the structure that falls
- Core: The Tower falls when a false structure can no longer stand. The lightning is a revelation that clears the ground.
- Symbolism: a tall tower struck by lightning, its crown falling, two people plunging, flames; sudden truth, collapse of illusion, and liberation through ruin.
- Upright: Expect or accept a sudden shake-up. A revelation changes the foundation. What falls was not built to last; let it clear the space.
- Reversed: A delayed collapse, internal upheaval, or fear of change. You may be postponing a necessary truth. The foundation still needs to be rebuilt.

## The Star
- Keywords: hope, healing, guidance, serenity, inspiration
- Number: 17 — the star after the storm
- Core: The Star appears after the Tower. Naked and unashamed, the figure pours water in trust; hope is quiet and complete.
- Symbolism: a kneeling woman pouring water from two jars, eight stars, a pool and a distant bird, a tree; renewal, generosity, and calm after devastation.
- Upright: Hope and healing are real. Follow the small light, restore faith, and give freely from what you have. This is a time of gentle renewal.
- Reversed: Lost hope, cynicism, or a temporary dimming of faith. You may doubt the light. Reconnect with what still nourishes you.

## The Moon
- Keywords: intuition, illusion, dreams, uncertainty, the unconscious
- Number: 18 — the dim light
- Core: The Moon lights the path only partly. Dreams and instincts speak, but not everything seen is true.
- Symbolism: a moon with a face, a tower on each side, a crayfish in a pool, a dog and a wolf howling, a path into the distance; imagination, danger, and instinct at twilight.
- Upright: Trust intuition but verify facts. Things are not fully revealed; confusion, dream-work, and hidden currents are present. Move carefully.
- Reversed: Emerging from confusion, facing a fear, or deception clearing. The fog lifts slowly. Distinguish real intuition from projected anxiety.

## The Sun
- Keywords: joy, clarity, vitality, success, truth
- Number: 19 — unclouded light
- Core: The Sun makes everything visible. Warmth, clarity, and honest happiness are available now.
- Symbolism: a radiant sun with a face, a joyful child on a white horse, a sunflower wall, open arms; vitality, innocence, and unguarded joy.
- Upright: Success, clarity, and warmth. Let yourself be seen and enjoy the moment. The answer is simple and in plain sight.
- Reversed: Temporary dimming, hidden disappointment, or modest rather than dramatic success. The sun is behind a cloud. Clarity returns with patience.

## Judgement
- Keywords: awakening, reckoning, renewal, calling, resurrection
- Number: 20 — the rising call
- Core: Judgement is the moment of reckoning and rebirth. An old story is assessed and a truer one begins.
- Symbolism: an angel with a trumpet, rising figures in graves, mountains and a vast sky; a call that summons the self to rise and be counted.
- Upright: A decisive awakening. You are called to re-evaluate, forgive, and step into a fuller life. Listen to what is asking to be born.
- Reversed: Avoidance of a necessary reckoning, self-criticism, or missing the call. You know what needs to change. Stop postponing the answer.

## The World
- Keywords: completion, integration, achievement, travel, wholeness
- Number: 21 — the circle closed
- Core: The World marks a completed cycle. The four directions are gathered; the work is integrated and can be celebrated.
- Symbolism: a dancing figure within a laurel wreath, the four fixed-sign symbols at the corners, a red scarf; totality, accomplishment, and the end of a long journey.
- Upright: A phase is complete. Celebrate, integrate the lesson, and prepare for the next cycle. You have earned this wholeness.
- Reversed: An unfinished ending, a loose thread, or reluctance to close a chapter. Something remains to be gathered before the next beginning.
