# Pentacles — RWS Meanings

Element: earth. Pentacles describe the body, work, money, resources, and what can be made real. Numbered cards show stages of material life; court cards show people or ways of expressing earth.

## Ace of Pentacles
- Keywords: new opportunity, abundance, manifestation, prosperity
- Core: A hand offers a pentacle from a cloud. A practical opportunity is planted and can grow.
- Symbolism: a large gold pentacle with a five-pointed star, a hand from a cloud, a walled garden, a river and mountains; seed of material plenty.
- Upright: A new resource, job, or financial start. Take the practical step; good things grow from a solid seed.
- Reversed: A delayed or missed opportunity, greed, or an insecure foundation. Check the value before you invest.

## Two of Pentacles
- Keywords: balance, juggling, adaptability, changing priorities
- Core: A figure dances with two pentacles. Life requires keeping several plates moving at once.
- Symbolism: a jovial figure juggling two coins with an infinity ribbon, ships on rough seas, hills; flexibility and shifting demands.
- Upright: Manage change with grace. Priorities shift; stay light on your feet rather than rigid.
- Reversed: Overwhelm, financial imbalance, or refusing to adapt. Simplify before something drops.

## Three of Pentacles
- Keywords: teamwork, craft, planning, recognition of skill
- Core: Three workers build together. Collaboration turns individual skill into something lasting.
- Symbolism: a master builder, a monk and a nun in a church, three pentacles, a planned arch; cooperation and skilled construction.
- Upright: Work well with others. A project benefits from shared expertise; value each person's craft.
- Reversed: Poor coordination, disregard for skill, or working in isolation. Align the team before building more.

## Four of Pentacles
- Keywords: security, possessiveness, thrift, control
- Core: A figure clutches four pentacles. Safety is real, but holding too tightly can freeze life.
- Symbolism: a seated figure hugging a pentacle with three more around him, a city behind; possessiveness and the fear of lack.
- Upright: Secure what you have, but notice where fear has turned into grasping. Generosity keeps wealth alive.
- Reversed: Release, overspending, or a loosened grip. You may be letting go of control in a healthy or a careless way.

## Five of Pentacles
- Keywords: hardship, loss, exclusion, needing help
- Core: Two figures trudge through snow past a lit window. Lack is real, and so is the possibility of help.
- Symbolism: a pair in rags in a snowstorm, a church window glowing with pentacles, a bandaged leg; poverty, cold, and unseen shelter.
- Upright: A difficult material time. Name the need and ask for support; the light is closer than it appears.
- Reversed: Recovery beginning, refusing help, or a slow return from loss. Accept the hand that is offered.

## Six of Pentacles
- Keywords: generosity, charity, balance of giving and receiving
- Core: A merchant gives coins to a beggar while scales hang in the air. Wealth is meant to circulate.
- Symbolism: a rich figure distributing coins, a kneeling recipient, balanced scales, a city gate; generosity, justice, and exchange.
- Upright: Give or receive with grace. A fair exchange, a loan, or help offered without strings. Notice where you stand in the flow.
- Reversed: Unequal power, strings attached, or stinginess. Check the real cost of the gift.

## Seven of Pentacles
- Keywords: assessment, patience, investment, slow growth
- Core: A figure rests on a hoe looking at growing vines. Work has been planted; now comes the waiting and review.
- Symbolism: a farmer beside seven pentacles on vines, a rising sun and distant fields; evaluation, patience, and the long term.
- Upright: Review what you have sown. Growth is happening slowly; adjust rather than uproot.
- Reversed: Impatience, a poor harvest, or a project that needs more time. Decide whether to keep tending or cut losses.

## Eight of Pentacles
- Keywords: skill, diligence, practice, mastery
- Core: A craftsperson works steadily on pentacles. Repetition turns ability into artistry.
- Symbolism: a worker carving coins, a row of finished pentacles, a town and a workshop; focused labour and the joy of craft.
- Upright: Do the work. Apprenticeship, practice, and careful attention are rewarded. Small steady gains add up.
- Reversed: Boredom, cutting corners, or a repetitive task without growth. Renew the purpose behind the labour.

## Nine of Pentacles
- Keywords: independence, abundance, self-sufficiency, refinement
- Core: A woman stands in a vineyard with a falcon. Comfort has been earned and can be enjoyed alone.
- Symbolism: a well-dressed figure in a garden of vines and nine pentacles, a hooded falcon, a walled estate; security, taste, and calm prosperity.
- Upright: Enjoy what you have built. Independence, financial ease, and the luxury of your own company.
- Reversed: Insecurity behind a polished surface, dependence, or a fear of sharing wealth. Check what true security requires.

## Ten of Pentacles
- Keywords: wealth, family legacy, stability, long-term security
- Core: A multi-generational family scene beneath ten pentacles. Resources are held across time, not just for one person.
- Symbolism: an old man with a dog, a couple and children, ten pentacles forming a tree of life, a great arch and city; legacy and enduring wealth.
- Upright: Lasting security. Family, inheritance, a strong foundation, or a structure that outlives you.
- Reversed: Family burden, unstable legacy, or disputes over resources. Build something that truly serves the line.

## Page of Pentacles
- Keywords: study, practical beginning, message, application
- Core: A young figure holds a pentacle and looks at it closely. Learning by doing begins here.
- Symbolism: a youthful figure in a flowering field with a pentacle, a ploughed field and distant hills; earnest study and a grounded start.
- Upright: A new course, job, or practical skill. Take the first concrete step and be willing to learn.
- Reversed: Procrastination, scattered effort, or an impractical plan. Ground the idea in daily action.
- Court perspective: The Page is the beginner of earth — serious, teachable, and finding their footing.

## Knight of Pentacles
- Keywords: reliability, steady progress, duty, patience
- Core: A knight sits motionless on a heavy horse holding a pentacle. Slow and dependable wins the long work.
- Symbolism: a armoured rider on a plough horse, a field and vines, a pentacle in hand; diligence, routine, and unhurried progress.
- Upright: Keep at it. Reliable effort, a steady job, or a task that rewards patience over speed.
- Reversed: Stagnation, stubborn routine, or a burden carried without joy. Check whether the pace still serves the goal.
- Court perspective: The Knight is earth in motion — slow, responsible, and unwilling to cut corners.

## Queen of Pentacles
- Keywords: nurture, practicality, resourcefulness, grounded care
- Core: The Queen holds a pentacle in a lush garden. She makes life comfortable and beautiful through practical care.
- Symbolism: a queen on a carved throne with a pentacle, a rabbit at her feet, flowers and a harvest; warmth, provision, and sensuous abundance.
- Upright: Care for the body and the home. Resourceful, generous, and steady — you make others feel safe.
- Reversed: Overextension, neglect of self, or possessive care. You cannot pour from an empty cup.
- Court perspective: The Queen is mature earth — able to nurture, manage, and enjoy what is real.

## King of Pentacles
- Keywords: prosperity, leadership, security, mastery of resources
- Core: The King sits on a throne of vines holding a pentacle. Wealth and status are used to build and protect.
- Symbolism: a king in a rich robe on a carved throne, a grapevine motif, a pentacle, a city and fields; material mastery and generous rule.
- Upright: Build something enduring. Strong management, financial wisdom, and the ability to provide for many.
- Reversed: Greed, rigidity, or a leader driven only by security. Wealth without generosity becomes a cage.
- Court perspective: The King is earth mastered — the capacity to create, hold, and wisely use real resources.
