# Swords — RWS Meanings

Element: air. Swords describe thought, communication, conflict, and clarity. Numbered cards show stages of the mind; court cards show people or ways of using intellect.

## Ace of Swords
- Keywords: clarity, truth, decisive thought, new idea
- Core: A new idea cuts through confusion. Truth is available if you can wield it cleanly.
- Symbolism: a hand from a cloud gripping an upright sword, a crown and laurel, distant mountains; victory of mind and clear-cut truth.
- Upright: A breakthrough in thinking. Name the truth, make the decision, and communicate it precisely.
- Reversed: Confusion, harsh words, or a truth that cuts rather than heals. Think before speaking.

## Two of Swords
- Keywords: blocked decision, withholding, inner conflict, balance
- Core: A blindfolded figure holds two swords. A decision is suspended because the heart is not ready to look.
- Symbolism: a blindfolded figure by the sea, two crossed swords, a crescent moon; deliberation and refused knowledge.
- Upright: A stalemate. You already know enough; remove the blindfold and choose.
- Reversed: A decision finally made, information received, or continued avoidance. The blockade lifts slowly.

## Three of Swords
- Keywords: heartbreak, painful truth, grief, separation
- Core: Swords pierce a heart in a storm. Truth can wound; grief has a place in clearing the air.
- Symbolism: a heart pierced by three swords, rain clouds, a barren landscape; sorrow, betrayal, and the pain of seeing clearly.
- Upright: Acknowledge the hurt. A painful truth, a breakup, or honest grief. Do not pretend the wound is not there.
- Reversed: Slow recovery, denial of pain, or a truth finally faced. Healing begins when the feeling is named.

## Four of Swords
- Keywords: rest, retreat, recovery, contemplation
- Core: A figure rests in a tomb-like chapel. Withdrawal is needed to restore the mind.
- Symbolism: a reclining figure above a stone tomb, four swords, a stained window with a figure; stillness, recovery, and sanctuary.
- Upright: Pause and recuperate. Sleep, solitude, or a period of quiet before the next demand.
- Reversed: Restlessness, returning too soon, or a retreat that has become avoidance. Rise when you are truly ready.

## Five of Swords
- Keywords: conflict, defeat, cunning, hollow victory
- Core: One figure gathers swords while others leave in loss. Winning at any cost leaves the field empty.
- Symbolism: a smirking figure holding three swords, two defeated figures, a stormy sky; dishonour, strife, and a bitter outcome.
- Upright: A conflict where nobody truly wins. Choose whether being right is worth the cost.
- Reversed: Shame, reconciliation, or a conflict finally laid down. Step out of the squaring match.

## Six of Swords
- Keywords: transition, leaving trouble, passage, moving on
- Core: A boat crosses calm water away from a storm. Change brings relief even when it is uncertain.
- Symbolism: a ferryman, a cloaked passenger, six swords in the boat, calm and rough waters; passage from turbulence to quieter seas.
- Upright: A necessary move. Leave the old conflict behind and cross to a steadier place.
- Reversed: A return to old problems, a delayed departure, or a passage that cannot be avoided. Pack with intention.

## Seven of Swords
- Keywords: strategy, deception, cunning, stealth
- Core: A figure slips away with swords. Not every act is honest; sometimes strategy requires moving quietly.
- Symbolism: a figure tiptoeing from a tent with five swords, three left behind, a bright moon; cunning, theft, and tactical retreat.
- Upright: Watch for hidden motives. A clever plan, a necessary secret, or a boundary against someone else's deceit.
- Reversed: Exposure, guilt, or a deception returned. Honesty is the only stable ground now.

## Eight of Swords
- Keywords: restriction, self-imposed limits, fear, helplessness
- Core: A bound, blindfolded figure stands among swords. Many restrictions are held in place by belief.
- Symbolism: a tied and blindfolded woman in a field of eight swords, a castle on a hill, a moon; paralysis, fear, and unseen ways out.
- Upright: The prison is partly mental. Identify the belief that holds you and take one small step out.
- Reversed: Emerging from limitation, naming the fear, or a restriction that lifts. Freedom starts with seeing clearly.

## Nine of Swords
- Keywords: anxiety, worry, nightmare, despair
- Core: A figure sits up in bed at night, face in hands. The mind has made a prison of fear.
- Symbolism: a seated figure in a nightshirt, nine swords on the wall, a moon and stars, quilts with roses; insomnia and anxious thought.
- Upright: Anxiety is real but often magnified in the dark. Name the fear; ask what is fact and what is imagined.
- Reversed: Nightmare easing, asking for help, or worry beginning to lift. Share the burden instead of carrying it alone.

## Ten of Swords
- Keywords: ending, betrayal, rock bottom, relief after pain
- Core: A figure lies pierced by ten swords beneath a dark sky. It is the end of a painful cycle; only rebuilding remains.
- Symbolism: a fallen figure with ten swords, a blood-red sky, a distant city, a hint of dawn; total ending and the first light after it.
- Upright: Something is definitively over. Allow the pain, then begin again; it cannot get worse from here.
- Reversed: Recovery beginning, a delayed ending, or refusing to admit the phase is finished. The dawn is already present.

## Page of Swords
- Keywords: curiosity, vigilance, sharp mind, messages
- Core: A youthful figure holds a sword and scans the horizon. Eager intelligence wants to test the world.
- Symbolism: a young figure in a windy landscape with a sword, birds and clouds, a hilltop; alertness and quick thinking.
- Upright: A message, a new line of inquiry, or a watchful mind. Ask questions, but temper sharp words with care.
- Reversed: Suspicion, gossip, or words used carelessly. Check the facts before acting on a suspicion.
- Court perspective: The Page is the beginner of thought — clever, restless, and still learning discretion.

## Knight of Swords
- Keywords: speed, assertion, intellect, confrontation
- Core: A knight charges with sword drawn. Thought becomes action, sometimes before feeling catches up.
- Symbolism: a rider on a rearing horse, a drawn sword, a stormy sky and birds; intellectual force and urgent movement.
- Upright: Act on an idea, speak your case, or move quickly. Make sure the haste has a worthy aim.
- Reversed: Recklessness, aggression, or a careless argument. Slow the mind before it cuts.
- Court perspective: The Knight is thought in motion — brilliant, impatient, and liable to run ahead of others.

## Queen of Swords
- Keywords: clarity, discernment, honesty, independence
- Core: The Queen holds a sword upright and a hand extended. She sees clearly and speaks the truth with composure.
- Symbolism: a queen on a throne between clouds, an upright sword, a hand raised, a bird and mountains; sharp intelligence and measured judgement.
- Upright: Use clear judgement. Say what needs to be said, keep your boundaries, and avoid needless cruelty.
- Reversed: Cynicism, harshness, or a truth used as a weapon. Soften the edge without losing clarity.
- Court perspective: The Queen is mature air — independent, perceptive, and honest without being unkind.

## King of Swords
- Keywords: authority, reason, truth, strategy
- Core: The King holds a double-edged sword. Reason and principle guide the judgement of others.
- Symbolism: a king on a throne between pillars, a double-edged sword, a moon and birds, a clouded sky; intellectual authority and rigorous truth.
- Upright: Decide on principle. A fair, rational judgement, a clear strategy, or sound advice. Listen before you rule.
- Reversed: Tyranny of logic, manipulation, or cold detachment. A clever argument is not the same as truth.
- Court perspective: The King is air mastered — the capacity to think clearly and judge fairly under pressure.
