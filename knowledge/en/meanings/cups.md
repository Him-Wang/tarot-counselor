# Cups — RWS Meanings

Element: water. Cups describe feelings, relationships, intuition, and the ways we give and receive love. Numbered cards show emotional stages; court cards show people or styles of relating.

## Ace of Cups
- Keywords: new love, emotional beginning, intuition, compassion
- Core: A new feeling overflows. Love, creativity, or a fresh emotional capacity is offered.
- Symbolism: a large cup with five streams, a dove with a wafer, a hand from a cloud, a host of lilies; an outpouring of grace and feeling.
- Upright: Open your heart. A new relationship, creative wellspring, or renewed compassion. Receive what is freely given.
- Reversed: Blocked feelings, emotional dryness, or love withheld. The cup is there but the flow is restricted.

## Two of Cups
- Keywords: partnership, mutual attraction, union, harmony
- Core: Two people exchange cups. A bond is formed on the basis of mutual regard.
- Symbolism: two figures exchanging cups, a winged lion head between them, a landscape and a flower; partnership, balance, and attraction.
- Upright: Mutual connection. A relationship, reconciliation, or a partnership based on respect. Both people choose the bond.
- Reversed: Imbalance, miscommunication, or a one-sided attachment. The exchange is not equal; name what is missing.

## Three of Cups
- Keywords: friendship, celebration, community, support
- Core: Three cups are raised together. Joy is shared and friendship is the ground of it.
- Symbolism: three dancing figures with raised cups, flowers and fruit, a bright sky; friendship, revelry, and communal delight.
- Upright: Celebrate with others. Friends, a social event, or a supportive circle. Shared joy multiplies the good.
- Reversed: Overindulgence, exclusion, or a friendship group that drains. Choose company that truly nourishes.

## Four of Cups
- Keywords: contemplation, dissatisfaction, withdrawal, missed offer
- Core: A figure sits beneath a tree with arms crossed while a cup is offered. Discontent can make us overlook what is given.
- Symbolism: a seated figure under a tree, three cups before him, a fourth offered from a cloud; apathy, reflection, and an unseen opportunity.
- Upright: Pause and reassess. You may be bored or disappointed, but an offer is present. Notice what you have been ignoring.
- Reversed: Emerging from withdrawal, accepting an offer, or a restless dissatisfaction that passes. Engagement returns slowly.

## Five of Cups
- Keywords: loss, grief, regret, what remains
- Core: Three cups are spilled; two stand. Grief is real, but the remaining cups are still yours.
- Symbolism: a cloaked figure looking at three fallen cups, two upright behind, a river and a distant castle; loss, mourning, and a path back.
- Upright: Name the loss and allow grief. Do not let sorrow hide what is still standing. The river offers a way through.
- Reversed: Acceptance, reluctant recovery, or refusal to grieve. You are beginning to turn toward what remains.

## Six of Cups
- Keywords: nostalgia, childhood, kindness, past patterns
- Core: The past comes gently into the present. Memory, innocence, and old gifts resurface.
- Symbolism: two children with cups, flowers in each, a wall and a distant house; childhood scenes, generosity, and remembering.
- Upright: A gentle link to the past. Old friends, family, or a pattern worth revisiting with kindness.
- Reversed: Living in the past, unresolved childhood material, or difficulty moving forward. Take the lesson, not the replay.

## Seven of Cups
- Keywords: choices, fantasy, illusion, many possibilities
- Core: Seven cups hold different promises, some real and some mirage. Too many options cloud the heart.
- Symbolism: a figure before seven floating cups with images of treasure, a snake, a castle, a jewel, a tower, a shroud, and a head; desire and illusion.
- Upright: Many possibilities, not all genuine. Sort fantasy from substance and choose one cup deliberately.
- Reversed: Clarification, a decision made, or confusion clearing. The fog of options begins to lift.

## Eight of Cups
- Keywords: leaving, disillusionment, searching, moving on
- Core: A figure walks away from eight cups toward mountains. Emotional courage is needed to leave what no longer fits.
- Symbolism: a departing figure, eight cups left behind, a moon and mountains; withdrawal from an unfulfilling situation toward deeper meaning.
- Upright: It is okay to leave. A search for something truer, even at the cost of what you have built.
- Reversed: Fear of leaving, circular return, or finally finding what was sought. Decide whether staying is still honest.

## Nine of Cups
- Keywords: contentment, wishes, satisfaction, emotional security
- Core: A seated figure rests before nine cups. Feelings are settled and a wish may be granted.
- Symbolism: a contented figure with a feathered cap, nine cups in a semicircle, a blue sky; well-being, comfort, and fulfilled desire.
- Upright: Enjoy contentment. A wish fulfilled, a sense of abundance, or simple emotional peace.
- Reversed: Empty satisfaction, greed, or a wish that did not bring happiness. Check what you actually need.

## Ten of Cups
- Keywords: family happiness, harmony, lasting love, fulfilment
- Core: Two figures raise cups toward a rainbow; children dance. The emotional ideal is shared and lasting.
- Symbolism: a rainbow with ten cups, a couple, dancing children, a cottage and green fields; family joy and enduring harmony.
- Upright: Deep relational happiness. Family, partnership, or a sense of belonging that lasts.
- Reversed: Family conflict, unmet ideal, or harmony that is only on the surface. Work on the real foundation.

## Page of Cups
- Keywords: emotional message, curiosity, sensitivity, new feeling
- Core: A young figure holds a cup with a fish peeking out. Feeling arrives in an unexpected, playful form.
- Symbolism: a youthful figure in a landscape, a cup with a fish, distant sea and flowers; innocent emotion and a surprising message.
- Upright: A tender message, a budding romance, or creative inspiration. Stay open to small, imaginative gestures.
- Reversed: Childishness, moodiness, or a message not meant seriously. Take the feeling gently, not literally.
- Court perspective: The Page is the beginner of feeling — curious, awkward, and sincere.

## Knight of Cups
- Keywords: romance, idealism, invitation, emotional movement
- Core: A knight rides slowly with a cup. He brings an offer of the heart, guided by feeling more than reason.
- Symbolism: a rider on a pale horse holding a cup, a winged helmet, a river and distant mountains; romantic pursuit and imaginative devotion.
- Upright: An invitation, romantic gesture, or a pursuit of meaning. Follow the heart, but keep your feet on the ground.
- Reversed: Unrealistic idealism, mood swings, or a promise made in fantasy. Check whether the feeling is reciprocal and practical.
- Court perspective: The Knight is feeling in motion — sincere, dreamy, and easily swept away.

## Queen of Cups
- Keywords: empathy, intuition, care, emotional depth
- Core: The Queen holds a cup and gazes into it. She understands feeling without needing to fix it.
- Symbolism: a queen on a shell throne by the sea, an ornate cup, a chalice with a cherub, waves and rocks; deep empathy and imaginative sensitivity.
- Upright: Lead with compassion. Trust intuition, listen deeply, and create a safe emotional space. Keep your own boundaries.
- Reversed: Emotional overwhelm, manipulation, or neglecting your own needs. Care for others without disappearing into them.
- Court perspective: The Queen is mature water — attentive, intuitive, and able to hold another's feeling steadily.

## King of Cups
- Keywords: emotional mastery, calm, wisdom, compassion
- Core: The King holds a cup steady on stormy water. He feels deeply but is not ruled by feeling.
- Symbolism: a king on a throne at sea, a hand on a cup, a ship and a fish motif, turbulent water; mastery of emotion with an open heart.
- Upright: Respond, don't react. A wise, calming presence, sound emotional judgement, and compassion with boundaries.
- Reversed: Volatility, suppression, or a manipulative calm. Feelings are either hidden or spilling out. Seek honest balance.
- Court perspective: The King is water mastered — the capacity to care deeply and remain steady.
