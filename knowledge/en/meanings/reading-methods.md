# Reading Methods

How to build an interpretation from card meanings, spread positions, and the client's situation.

## Four ways to read a reversed card
1. Blocked: the energy is present but stuck; the upright meaning is delayed or obstructed.
2. Internalised: the energy turns inward; what looked like external action is now a private process.
3. Excess or lack: too much of the quality, or a deficiency of it; the remedy is proportion.
4. Adjustment signal: a small nudge to modify the approach, not a total reversal.
Do not automatically read every reversal as negative; choose the reading that fits the surrounding cards.

## Standard position meanings
- Single card: the card's core theme, upright or reversed, applied to the whole question.
- Three-card spread: past / present / future, or situation / action / outcome. Name which system you are using before reading.
- Celtic Cross: the full positional scheme (present, challenge, unconscious, past, conscious, near future, self, environment, hopes/fears, outcome) adds layers of context; keep each position to one clear sentence before linking them.

## Mandatory four-step order (do not skip)
1. Base meaning: what does this card traditionally say, upright or reversed?
2. Position: what does this slot in the spread ask of the card?
3. Question: how does that answer the specific question asked?
4. Situation: what does this mean in the client's real circumstances, with their own words?
Say the steps in this order; jumping from card name to prediction is the most common error.

## Taboos
- No absolute predictions about health, legal outcomes, or another person's choices.
- Do not diagnose or prescribe medical, legal, or financial action.
- Do not claim a card reveals hidden facts about third parties.
- Do not use a spread to pressure or control the client; the client keeps agency.
- Do not stack catastrophic cards into a fate; describe tendencies and invite reflection.
