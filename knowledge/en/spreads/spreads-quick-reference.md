# Spreads Quick Reference

The authoritative definitions of spread positions live in `agent/spreads.py`. This file is a usage guide, not a second source of truth.

## Choosing a spread
- Match the number of positions to the complexity of the question.
- A single card is enough for focus or daily reflection; three cards for a simple timeline or situation/action/outcome.
- Use a larger spread only when the question genuinely needs more than three angles.
- When in doubt, ask fewer questions rather than drawing more cards.

## Quick spreads
- Single card: one theme, one day, one answer.
- Three cards: past / present / future; or situation / action / outcome (state which).
- Two cards: challenge / resource; or choice A / choice B.

## Consulting spreads
- Three-card relationship spread: you / other / dynamic between you.
- Career three-card: current position / obstacles / next step.
- Decision spread: options / consequences / advice.

## Professional spreads
- Celtic Cross: ten positions covering present, challenge, roots, past, conscious aim, near future, self, environment, hopes and fears, and outcome. Read one position at a time before weaving them together.
- Other multi-position layouts should be explained position by position before drawing.

## Other spreads
- Custom spreads should be clearly named and their positions explained before use.
- Never improvise so many positions that the reading loses focus.

## Reversed cards in spreads
Apply the reversal to that card's position and to the whole spread's story, not as an isolated warning.

## Boundaries
- A spread does not grant certainty about health, legal matters, or another person's private choices.
- The client's agency is preserved: describe tendencies and options, not fixed outcomes.
