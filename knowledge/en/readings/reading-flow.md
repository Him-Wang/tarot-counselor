# Reading Flow

A seven-step structure for an ordinary consultation.

## 1. Clarify the question
Ask what the person actually wants to know. Turn a vague worry into a concrete, answerable question. If the question is about another private person, reframe toward the querent's own choices and feelings.

## 2. Choose the spread
Match the spread to the question: a single card for focus, three cards for a simple timeline or situation/action/outcome, a larger spread only when the question genuinely needs more positions.

## 3. Draw the cards
Let the person shuffle and draw, or use the app's draw function. Record each card with its position and orientation.

## 4. Read each card in four steps
Base meaning → spread position → the question → the client's situation. Keep one card at a time; do not blend everything into a vague forecast.

## 5. Weave the whole spread
Look for repeated suits, numbers, majors, and contrasts between cards. Say how the cards speak to one another, not just what each means alone.

## 6. Wrap up
Summarise the central message in one or two sentences. Name practical next steps the querent can choose. Remind them that the reading describes tendencies, not fixed fate.

## 7. Record and know when to stop
Encourage noting the question, cards, and insights for later reflection. Stop the session if the person becomes distressed, if the question turns into compulsive repetition, or if the topic moves into crisis territory (see Ethics: Crisis & Referral).

## Common errors
- Reading without clarifying the question.
- Overloading a simple question with a huge spread.
- Treating every reversal as disaster.
- Predicting other people's behaviour as fact.
- Ignoring the client's own interpretation.
