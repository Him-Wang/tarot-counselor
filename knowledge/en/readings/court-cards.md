# Court Cards

## Four usages
1. A real person in the querent's life.
2. An aspect of the querent's own personality.
3. An attitude or role to adopt (advice).
4. A message or a stage of development.

## Ranks: maturity and shadow
- Page: the beginner — curious, eager, inexperienced. Shadow: restlessness, immaturity.
- Knight: the mover — driven, passionate, one-pointed. Shadow: recklessness, impatience.
- Queen: the mature receiver — containing, intuitive, relational. Shadow: possessiveness, manipulation.
- King: the mature director — authoritative, wise, responsible. Shadow: rigidity, domination.

## The four elements in court cards
- Wands: fire — action and charisma.
- Cups: water — feeling and intuition.
- Swords: air — thought and communication.
- Pentacles: earth — practicality and resources.

## Relationship readings
A court card may describe the querent, the other person, or the dynamic between them. Ask which role fits rather than assuming it is always a third party.

## Reversed and shadow expressions
A reversed court card may point to an immature or unbalanced expression, a quality turned inward, or a person who is not acting from their best self. Speak about behaviour, not about a person's worth.

## Misconceptions
- Court cards are not always literal people.
- A court card is not a fixed personality type assigned to someone forever.
- Age and gender in the image are symbolic roles, not literal descriptions.

## Three questions to ask
1. Is this card a person, a part of the querent, or a role to step into?
2. Which element and rank are most visible in the situation?
3. What would the mature version of this card do here?
