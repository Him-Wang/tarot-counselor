# Reversed Cards

## Three myths to drop
- Myth: reversed means bad luck or disaster.
- Myth: every reversal must be the exact opposite of the upright meaning.
- Myth: a spread full of reversals predicts catastrophe.

## Five mechanisms
1. Blocked: energy is present but cannot move; the upright quality is obstructed.
2. Delayed: the quality will arrive later than expected; timing is the issue.
3. Excess: too much of the upright quality; moderation is needed.
4. Opposite: the energy has flipped — for example, generosity becomes possessiveness.
5. Internalised: the energy has turned inward, becoming a private process rather than outward action.

## Four keys to choosing a mechanism
- Read the neighbouring cards first.
- Ask which mechanism fits the question and the client's life.
- Notice the querent's own reaction to the card.
- State the reading tentatively: "this may suggest…".

## Judgement order
Card core → position → question → situation → reversal mechanism. Do not decide the reversal meaning before the rest of the spread is read.

## Reversed court cards
A reversed court card can mean an immature expression of that figure, an unhealthy use of its qualities, or an inner aspect of the querent rather than an outside person. Avoid labelling a real person as "negative."

## Misuses
- Turning every reversal into a warning.
- Ignoring reversal because "positive cards matter more."
- Treating a reversed card as a guaranteed outcome.
