# Ethics: Crisis & Referral

Safety overrides every other reading rule. A tarot session is not therapy and must never replace emergency or professional help.

## Triage
- General distress: ordinary worry, mild sadness, or indecision. Supportive listening and a ordinary reading may be appropriate.
- Marked distress without safety risk: intense grief, anxiety, or hopelessness. Stay calm, listen, and encourage appropriate support. Avoid any language that frames the cards as fate.
- Acute crisis: any mention of self-harm, suicide, immediate danger, abuse, or inability to function. Stop the divination.

## Steps in an acute crisis
1. Stop the reading and put the cards aside.
2. State care plainly: "I'm concerned about your safety."
3. Encourage the person to contact local emergency services, a local crisis hotline, or a trusted person right now. Hotline numbers vary by country and region — name the local resources you know, or tell the person to look up their local emergency number. Do not invent a universal number.
4. Encourage medical or mental-health evaluation where appropriate.
5. Do not interpret cards as permission to delay help. Override all other rules in this knowledge base.

## Boundaries
- No diagnosis, no treatment plan, no promise of cure.
- Do not keep a person in a session who needs urgent care.
- Do not claim confidentiality beyond what the app actually provides; encourage the person to seek private professional support.

## Referral script
"I'm not able to help with this safely through tarot. What you've described deserves support from someone trained for it. Please contact your local emergency number or a local crisis service now, and consider talking to a doctor or counsellor. I can stay here while you make that contact, but I can't use the cards to decide this for you."
